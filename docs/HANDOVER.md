# Looking after the DG Events website

Welcome. This guide is for whoever keeps the Dormi Guide Events website up to
date. It assumes you have never used a content management system before, and it
does not assume you know anything about code. You do not need to.

Take it a section at a time. Nothing here can break the website, and there is a
note at the end about who to ask when something looks wrong.

---

## What you actually have

There are two separate things, and it helps to keep them apart in your head.

**The website** is what the public sees. You never edit it directly.

**The Studio** is your private control room. You log in, change words and
photos, press Publish, and the website updates itself. That is the only place
you need to go.

Think of it like a noticeboard. The Studio is the back of the board where you
pin things up. The website is the front, where people read them.

> **Your Studio address:** [(https://dormi-guide-events.sanity.studio/)]
>
> This needs filling in before you use this guide — see *Before you start*
> at the very bottom. Write it down somewhere you will find it again.

## Logging in

1. Open the Studio address in a browser. Chrome on a laptop is the easiest
   place to work, though it does function on a phone at a push.
2. Choose a way to sign in — Google is usually simplest, and works with a
   Gmail address.
3. That is it. There is no separate password to remember for the Studio itself.

If you see *"You do not have access to this project"*, your email address has
not been added yet. That is a two-minute job for the developer — ask them.

**A note on who can get in.** Only people invited to the project can see or
change anything. If someone leaves the organisation, ask for their access to be
removed. Do not share one login between several people; separate invitations
cost nothing and mean you can see who changed what.

## Finding your way around

Down the left you will see a list. It is deliberately in the order you will
use it most:

| | What it holds |
|---|---|
| **Events** | Every event, past and future. This is where you will spend most of your time. |
| **Gallery** | Photographs from events. |
| **Team** | The people on your About page. |
| **Sectors** | The four guides — Students, Graduates, Workers, Entrepreneur. You will rarely touch these. |
| **Site settings** | Your phone number, email, address and social links. |

Click any of these to see what is already there. Click an item to open it.

Two buttons matter, and they are at the bottom of the screen when something is
open:

- **Publish** puts your changes on the live website.
- If you close something without publishing, your typing is still saved as a
  draft. Drafts are private — the public cannot see them. You will see the word
  *Draft* or an orange dot next to anything unpublished.

That last point is worth repeating, because it is genuinely useful: **you can
start writing an event now, leave it half-finished for a week, and nobody sees
it until you press Publish.**

## Posting a new event

Open **Events**, then click the pencil-and-paper icon at the top to create a
new one. You will see a form. Here is every field, in plain terms.

### Event name
What you would call it on a poster. *Career Path Conference 2026.*

Keep it the real name of the event rather than a description of it.

### Web address
This becomes the link people share — the tail end of it, anyway.

Press **Generate** and it fills itself in from the event name. That is normally
all you need to do. If it produces something very long, you can shorten it by
hand; stick to lowercase letters and hyphens instead of spaces.

⚠️ **Once you have shared a link publicly, do not change this.** Changing it
breaks every link already out there, in every WhatsApp group and on every
poster with a QR code.

### Sector
Which of the four guides this event belongs to. Pick from the list.

This controls where the event shows up on the site — an event tagged *Dormi
Students Guide* appears on the Students page as well as in the main events list.

### Starts
The date and time it begins. Use the little calendar.

This one field does more work than it looks. **The website works out entirely
on its own whether an event is upcoming or finished, by comparing this date to
today.** There is nothing to switch over afterwards. The morning after an event
runs, it quietly moves itself from "Coming up" to "Past events". You never have
to remember to do it.

### Ends
Optional. Fill it in if the event has a set finishing time, leave it empty if
it does not. For a two-day event, put the finishing time on the second day and
the site will show it as a date range.

### Venue
The building or place. *Accra International Conference Centre.*

### City or town
Pre-filled with Accra, since most events are there. Change it when they are not.

### Cover photo
The main picture. It appears on the event card, at the top of the event page,
and — importantly — as the picture in the preview when someone shares the link.

Landscape photos (wider than tall) work best. There is guidance on sizes further
down.

After you upload, you will be asked for a **Photo description**. This is
required, and there is a good reason for it — see *About photos* below.

You can also drag the little circle on the photo to choose which part matters
most. When the site has to crop the picture to fit a narrow phone screen, it
keeps that part in view. Put it on the faces.

### Short summary
**This is the most important box on the page, and the one most people rush.**

One or two sentences. It appears:

- on the event card in the events list
- in Google search results
- **and as the text underneath the link when someone shares it on WhatsApp**

That last one is why it matters so much here. Most people will meet this event
as a link in a group chat. They will see the event name, this summary, and the
cover photo. That is the whole advert. If the summary is empty or unclear, the
link looks like spam and nobody taps it.

**What makes a good one.** Say what someone gets out of coming, in the plainest
words you have. Be concrete.

> ✅ *"A full day of honest conversations with people already doing the work, so
> you can choose a course knowing where it actually leads."*

> ✅ *"Thirty employers hiring now, plus CV surgeries and mock interviews running
> all day."*

> ❌ *"Join us for an exciting and impactful event!"* — says nothing.
>
> ❌ *"Career Path Conference 2026"* — just repeats the name.
>
> ❌ *"awf"* — placeholder text that will end up in a WhatsApp group.

Keep it under about 160 characters. The Studio will warn you if you go over,
because anything longer gets cut off with a "…" in search results and previews.

⚠️ **Get this right before you share the link anywhere.** WhatsApp remembers
the preview for a link the first time anyone posts it, and holds onto it for
one to three days. There is no way to force it to look again. So if you publish
with a summary of "test" and immediately paste the link into a group, that
group sees "test" for the next few days even after you fix it. Write the
summary, publish, *then* share.

### Full description
The main write-up on the event page, where you have room to breathe. Who it is
for, what happens, who is speaking, what to bring.

The toolbar lets you make headings, bold text, bullet points and links. Use
headings and bullets to break up anything longer than a few paragraphs — people
skim on phones.

### How to attend
People book by contacting you, not through the website. There is no sign-up
form, no basket, no payments — by design.

So this field is where you tell them exactly what to do. Be specific:

> *"Call 053 259 2824 to reserve a seat. Entry is free for students in uniform."*

This appears in a highlighted panel on the event page, because it is the thing
you actually want people to act on.

### Feature on the home page
A switch. Turn it on to pin this event to the top of the home page. Use it
sparingly — if everything is featured, nothing is.

### Then press Publish
The green button at the bottom. Your event is live.

## Adding photos to the gallery

Open **Gallery** and create a new item. There are only four things to fill in:

- **Photo** — the picture itself. Same crop circle as before; drag it onto what
  matters.
- **Photo description** — required. Say what is happening. See below.
- **Caption** — optional, shown under the photo. Leave it empty if the picture
  speaks for itself.
- **Taken at** — optional. Link the photo to the event it came from. Doing this
  means visitors can filter the gallery by event, which is genuinely useful once
  you have a few hundred photos.
- **Date taken** — sorts the gallery, newest first.

Upload photos one at a time. It is a little laborious for a big batch, but it
means every photo gets a proper description rather than a filename.

## Team members

Open **Team**.

- **Full name** and **Role** — as you would introduce them.
- **Photo** — a head-and-shoulders shot. Square or portrait works best. Use the
  crop circle to keep the face centred.
- **Short bio** — two or three sentences on who they are and what they do here.
  Warm and plain. This is not a CV.
- **Order on the page** — a number. **1** appears first, then 2, then 3.

### Reordering people
Change the numbers. That is the whole mechanism, and it is deliberately dull
because dull things do not break.

If you want to move someone from fourth to first, give them **1** and bump the
others down. If you find yourself renumbering constantly, a tip: number them
10, 20, 30, 40 instead of 1, 2, 3, 4. Then slotting someone new into the middle
is just giving them 25, and you never have to touch anyone else.

Anyone without a number ends up at the end, in no particular order.

## Contact details and social links

Open **Site settings**. Unlike everything else, there is only ever one of these
— you cannot create a second by accident, and you cannot delete it.

It has two tabs:

**Contact details**
- **Email address** — shown in the footer and on the contact page.
- **Phone number** — write it the way you would say it out loud, spaces and
  brackets included: *+233 (0) 53 259 2824*. The website turns it into a
  tappable dialling link by itself.
- **Address** — where you are based, one line per line.

**Social links**
Add one row per account you actually use. Choose the platform from the dropdown
and paste the full web address of your profile — the whole thing, starting with
`https://`.

Only add accounts you genuinely post on. Anything you leave out simply does not
appear on the site, which is much better than a dead link to an empty page.

Changing your phone number here changes it everywhere on the site at once. You
do not need to hunt through pages.

## Taking an event down

You have two options, and they are not the same.

### Unpublish — the one you almost always want
Open the event, click the small arrow next to the Publish button, and choose
**Unpublish**.

The event disappears from the website immediately, but everything you wrote is
kept. You can publish it again later with one click. Use this for an event
that has been postponed, or one you published too early.

### Delete — permanent
Same menu, choose **Delete**. This throws the event away for good, along with
its write-up and its photo.

**Our advice: almost never delete an event.** Past events are not clutter, they
are evidence — proof to a sceptical parent or a potential sponsor that you
actually run these things. The website sorts them into "Past events" on its own.
Leave them there.

Only delete something created by genuine mistake, like a duplicate or a test.

## What happens when you press Publish

**The website updates straight away.** There is no build to wait for, no button
for a developer to press. The site asks the Studio for the latest content every
time someone visits, so the next person to load the page sees your change.

Two things lag slightly, and neither needs any action from you:

| | How long |
|---|---|
| Pages on the website | Immediately |
| The preview for a link **already shared** | Up to 10 minutes |
| The list of pages given to Google | Up to an hour |

And one thing that lags much longer, which is worth knowing about:

⚠️ **WhatsApp remembers link previews for one to three days.** The first time
anyone posts a link in a chat, WhatsApp fetches the title, summary and photo and
keeps them. If you change the summary afterwards, chats that already have the
link keep showing the old one until WhatsApp forgets. Nobody can clear it — not
you, not us, not WhatsApp support.

The practical rule: **finish the event properly, publish, check it looks right,
and only then share the link.**

If you want to check before sharing, open the event page on the website and
read it as a stranger would.

## What must never go in the Studio

This is the one section to read twice.

**Everything you publish is public.** Not just the pages — the underlying
content itself can be read by anyone who knows where to look. This is a normal
and deliberate arrangement for a public website, and it is what makes the site
fast and cheap to run. But it means the Studio is **not** a private filing
cabinet.

Never put any of the following in:

- 🚫 **Anyone's personal details** — attendee names, phone numbers, emails,
  ages, school names. If a parent gave you their number, it does not go here.
- 🚫 **Anything financial** — bank details, mobile money numbers you use for
  receiving payments, sponsor contracts, budgets, invoices.
- 🚫 **Passwords or login details** of any kind, for anything, ever.
- 🚫 **Photos of children without permission from a parent or guardian.** Get
  consent before the photo goes anywhere near the Studio, and keep a record of
  it somewhere else.
- 🚫 **Internal notes.** "Speaker was difficult, do not invite again" is not a
  caption. There is no private-comment field. If you would not print it on a
  poster, it does not belong here.
- 🚫 **Draft content you consider sensitive.** Drafts are not shown on the
  website, but treat them as a convenience rather than a vault.

A simple test before typing anything: *would I be comfortable if this appeared
on the front page tomorrow?* If not, it goes in an email or a notebook instead.

## About photos

Photos are the single biggest thing you control that affects how the site feels
and how fast it loads.

### Rough sizes
You do not need to be precise. Aim for:

| Where | Shape | Rough size |
|---|---|---|
| Event cover photo | Landscape (wider than tall) | around 1600 pixels wide |
| Gallery photo | Any shape | around 1600 pixels on the longest side |
| Team photo | Square or portrait | around 800 pixels wide |

A photo straight off a modern phone is usually 3000–4000 pixels wide — larger
than needed, but **upload it anyway**. The website automatically makes smaller
copies for smaller screens, and a phone visitor only ever downloads the small
one. What you should avoid is going the other way: a small, blurry photo pulled
off Facebook cannot be improved, and it will look soft on a laptop.

### Why file size matters more here than elsewhere
Most of your visitors are on a phone, paying for data by the megabyte. A page
heavy with photos costs them real money, and on a slow connection they will
leave before it finishes loading.

The website already does a lot about this on its own: it converts your photos to
a more efficient format, makes several sizes, and holds off loading anything
below the fold until it is scrolled towards. You do not have to think about any
of that.

The one thing that helps: **do not upload photos larger than about 5MB**, and
prefer JPG over PNG for photographs. A screenshot saved as a PNG can be ten
times the size of the same picture as a JPG for no visible benefit.

### Why the photo description is required
Every photo asks for a description before you can save. It is the one field
people are tempted to fill with "photo" or "logo". Please do not — here is what
it is actually for.

**It is read aloud to blind visitors.** Someone using a screen reader hears your
description instead of seeing the photo. "Logo" tells them nothing. "Students
talking with a panellist between sessions" tells them what they are missing.

**It appears when a photo fails to load** — a real occurrence on a patchy
connection, which is a lot of your audience.

**Google reads it** to understand what your photos show, which helps people find
you.

Write what you would say if you were describing the photo to someone on the
phone:

> ✅ *"Students queuing at employer stands with folders of CVs"*
>
> ✅ *"A founder mid-pitch on a small stage while investors take notes"*
>
> ❌ *"Logo"* · *"An event"* · *"IMG_4032"* · *"photo of event"*

Skip "a picture of" — the screen reader already says it is an image. Just
describe what is happening.

## When something looks wrong

### Try this first
1. **Reload the page.** Genuinely — this fixes most things.
2. **Check it is published.** An orange dot or the word *Draft* means it is
   still private. Press Publish.
3. **Check the date.** An event that is not in "Coming up" has usually got a
   start date in the past. It will be further down under past events.
4. **Wait two minutes and reload.** Occasionally a change takes a moment.

### Content questions — ask your team
Anything about *what to say* rather than *how the site works*:

- what to write in a summary
- which sector an event belongs to
- whether a photo is suitable to publish
- who should be listed on the team page

These are yours to decide. This guide is here to help, not to rule.

### Technical problems — ask the developer
Get in touch if:

- the Studio will not load, or refuses your login
- you published something and it genuinely is not showing after a few reloads
- the contact form is not delivering messages
- something looks broken, overlapping or unreadable on the site
- you need someone added to or removed from the Studio
- you want a new kind of content that does not fit the boxes above

**Developer:** Randy Boateng

**Email:** randyboateng420@gmail.com

When reporting a problem, three things make it far quicker to fix:

1. **The web address** of the page — copy it from the browser bar.
2. **What you expected** versus what you saw.
3. **A screenshot.** On Windows press `Windows + Shift + S`; on a Mac
   `Cmd + Shift + 4`; on a phone, the usual screenshot buttons.

"The events page is broken" takes a long time to chase. "On
dgevents.com/events, the Pitch Night card shows no photo — screenshot attached"
is often fixed in minutes.
